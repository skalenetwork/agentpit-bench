# YouTube Shorts: the daily film on a second platform (spec)

Status: v1 draft · 2026-10-12 · not built. Sources were fetched 2026-10-12 and are listed at the end with each page's
own "last updated" date. Builds on two things now on `main`:
- **Match of the Day v0.4**: `motd.py`, merged in `6620bc302`, and switched on in `bench.toml` by `9ea90fa05`;
- **x-analytics**: `xstats.py`, the `x-metrics` loop and the 08:00 UTC digest, merged in `cdecd822a`.

## Goal

Each day's Match of the Day film also goes out as a YouTube Short. On X a post fades within a day. On YouTube a Short
keeps being found through search and the Shorts feed for months, the same compounding that `seo-spec.md` is after,
and the 9:16 file already exists. This spec adds no new rendering and no new facts. It covers upload, metadata built
from the film record, a link back to the round page, and metrics that feed the same tables and digest as X.

## Non-goals (v1)

- **Only the daily film.** Per-round results clips and the weekly race video are 16:9 (1200×675) and about 6 s long.
  A 16:9 upload is a normal video, not a Short (see §1), and five thin clips a day would bury the film. Later: a
  9:16 race render using the film template's `tall` layout would qualify.
- No 16:9 long-form uploads, no YouTube embed on our pages (pages stay first-party and under 100 KB; the round page
  keeps the self-hosted 16:9 film), no localised titles (v2: `localizations` from the i18n tables), no
  monetisation or YouTube Partner Program application, no paid promotion.
- No model-written text. Titles, descriptions, tags and the comment are templates filled from `film.json` and the
  round export. That's the film's own v0.4 rule, extended to its metadata.
- No new A/B slot (§9).

## 1. What a Short is, and why the film qualifies

- A video uploaded on or after 2024-10-15 that is square or vertical and **up to 3 minutes** long is a Short
  (YouTube Help 15424877). There is no API flag and no `#Shorts` tag is needed. The film is 9:16 (1080×1920),
  45–60 s, H.264 + AAC, and about 10 MB or less: `cards/motd/<date>/motd-<date>-tall.mp4`.
- The same MP4 file that X gets is uploaded unchanged. Its closing CTA ("reply to the next round's reveal post on
  @agentpitbench") still works for a YouTube viewer, since it points to X.
- **Music:** the bed is `static/motd/theme.m4a`. It's generated procedurally by `music.py` and dedicated to the public
  domain under CC0, so there's no Content ID claim to expect. If a claim does arrive, that's an incident: dispute it
  with `MUSIC-LICENSE.txt`.

## 2. Metadata (`agentpitbench/youtube.py`, pure builders)

Every string is built from `film.json` (`date`, `round_id`, `question`, `winner`, `hook`, `score`, `duration_s`,
`files.tall`) and the same `exports.export` round record the film was scripted from. Numbers are formatted with the
film's own helpers (`motd._cents`, `_signed`, `_pct`), so the description and the film can't disagree. Language is
**English**, matching X and the film's captions: `snippet.defaultLanguage = "en"`, and no audio language is set
because there's no speech.

Limits from the `videos` resource (2026-10-08): title ≤ 100 characters, description ≤ 5000 **bytes**, tags ≤ 500
characters in total (counting commas, and quotes around tags that contain spaces). `<` and `>` aren't allowed in
the title or description. The builders strip `<` and `>` and enforce the limits with `twitter.clip`-style
clipping. A test covers each limit.

**Title:** `"{hook} | {question}"`. The question is clipped first, then the hook. Example shape (not real data): "3 AIs said
Yes. Grok said No. | Will Bitcoin close above $120k on Oct 11?" The hook is the film's own scene-1 line. It names the
picks (outcome labels) but never says who was right, so the title adds no outcome beyond the film's opening frame.

**Description** (fixed lines, in this order; empty lines are dropped):

```
{question}
Result: {winner}.
{Agent} ({model line}): {pick} at {price} → {P&L} tokens        (one line per agent; "no bet ({reason})" if none)
Why this round: {score breakdown, signal names and points, from film.json score}
{EXHIBITION: pre-season / summoned / High-Stakes round, not counted in the season}   (only when exhibition)
Every pick, price and verbatim transcript: {round_url_utm}
Leaderboard and methodology (v{methodology_version}): https://agentpitbench.org/leaderboard/
AgentpitBench pits Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Grok against each other on real prediction markets.
Who runs it and how it stays independent: https://agentpitbench.org/independence/
Music: original, generated by code in the AgentpitBench repository, CC0.
Follow the live rounds on X: @agentpitbench
#AgentpitBench #AI #PredictionMarkets
```

- The first three hashtags show above the title, and these three are fixed. Agent names come from `AGENT_NAMES`
  (codex is shown as ChatGPT).
- **Hard rule: YouTube metadata links only `agentpitbench.org`.** It never links `agentpit.dev`, a market URL or
  `market_link()`. YouTube's regulated-goods policy bars directing viewers to online gambling sites that Google hasn't
  certified, and that includes URLs and links in text (Help 9229611; enforcement tightened 2025-03-19 and 2025-11-17
  per third-party coverage). The SKALE Labs / agentpit disclosure stays on `/independence/`, which the description links.
  A test asserts that the only host in any generated text is `agentpitbench.org`.

**Tags:** `AgentpitBench, AI benchmark, AI forecasting, prediction markets, Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok`, plus
`virality.category(question)` (e.g. `Crypto`). All are fixed or computed; nothing is invented.

**Other fields:** `categoryId = "28"` (Science & Technology; `yt-check` confirms it with `videoCategories.list`,
1 unit), `status.selfDeclaredMadeForKids = false` (a required audience declaration; betting content isn't
child-directed), `status.containsSyntheticMedia = false` (§6), `status.privacyStatus = youtube_privacy` (§4),
`status.embeddable = true`, `status.publicStatsViewable = true`, `notifySubscribers = true`. No custom thumbnail
(`poster.png` comes from the wide render, and the API's thumbnail support for Shorts isn't confirmed).

**Links back, with UTM.** `round_url_utm = {site_url}/round/{N}/?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=motd&utm_content={date}`.
This is the same shape as `motd.link_text` (`utm_source=x`). Links in Shorts descriptions and comments **aren't
clickable**: YouTube's anti-spam change of 2023-08-31 (third-party coverage), and the `videos` doc (2026-10-08) says
description URLs render as links only once the channel meets requirements such as verification or Advanced Features.
So the round URL appears in four places:
1. the description (full UTM URL, clickable if the channel ever qualifies);
2. the comment (short readable form `agentpitbench.org/round/N`);
3. the film's credibility strip;
4. the channel's header links (clickable; §5).

**Measurement** follows the share-everywhere decision: Cloudflare Web Analytics referrer domains (`youtube.com`,
`m.youtube.com`, `youtu.be`). UTM parameters are kept but not reported.

**Comment (the "pinned comment"):** after the video is first seen **public**, the bench posts one top-level comment
as the channel (`commentThreads.insert`, 50 units, scope `youtube.force-ssl`). Comments on a private video aren't
attempted.

```
Every pick, price and transcript from this round: agentpitbench.org/round/{N}
Why this round: {top signal and points}. Pick against the AIs: reply to the next reveal post on X, @agentpitbench.
```

The API has no pin method (`commentThreads` supports only `list` and `insert`). Pinning is an optional manual click
in YouTube Studio, and nothing depends on it. `youtube_comment = true` turns the comment on.

## 3. Upload flow (YouTube Data API v3)

**Client:** plain `requests`, which is already installed through `requests-oauthlib`. No `google-api-python-client`.

**Auth:**
- The OAuth 2.0 installed-app ("Desktop") client uses the loopback redirect (installed-apps guide, 2026-09-14).
  Refresh tokens are always returned for installed apps.
- `agentpitbench yt-login` copies the `x-login` flow for a headless server. It prints the consent URL with a
  `http://127.0.0.1:<port>` redirect. The operator approves in any browser and **chooses the brand channel** on the
  account picker. The browser then fails to load the loopback page, so the operator pastes the address-bar URL back
  with `yt-login --url '…'`.
- **Scopes:**
  - `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.force-ssl`: `videos.insert` accepts it, and `commentThreads.insert`,
    `videos.update` and `videos.list` of our own private status need it;
  - `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/yt-analytics.readonly`: reports.
- **Channel guard:** after login, and before every upload, `channels.list?mine=true` (1 unit, cached per process)
  must return `youtube_channel_id`. On a mismatch the upload is refused and logged at ERROR, so a token minted for a
  personal channel can never post there.

**Secrets** follow `config.py`'s convention:
- `YT_CLIENT_ID` and `YT_CLIENT_SECRET` go in `~/.config/agentpitbench/secrets.env` (loaded by `load_secrets`;
  real env vars win).
- The token chain lives at `SECRETS_FILE.parent / "yt_oauth2.json"`, overridable with `BENCH_YT_TOKEN_FILE`, the
  same pattern as `twitter.token_file()`. It's written atomically with mode 0600 (the `OAuth2Token._save` pattern).
- Nothing goes in `bench.toml` or the repo.

**Resumable upload** (Google's resumable-upload guide, 2026-09-14):
1. `POST https://www.googleapis.com/upload/youtube/v3/videos?uploadType=resumable&part=snippet,status&notifySubscribers=true`
   with the JSON video resource and the headers `X-Upload-Content-Length` and `X-Upload-Content-Type: video/mp4`.
   The `Location` header is the session URI. It's stored in `yt_videos.upload_session` **before** any bytes are
   sent.
2. `PUT` the whole file (about 10 MB, so one request). A `201` returns the video resource: store `video_id`, clear
   the session and set `state='uploaded'`.
3. **Recovery** after a lost connection, 500, 502, 503 or 504:
   - Send an empty `PUT` with `Content-Range: bytes */{size}`.
   - A `308` with `Range: bytes=0-k` means resume from `k+1`. A `308` without `Range` means start from 0.
   - A `200` or `201` means it's done: read `video_id` from that response. This is how a crash after YouTube
     accepted the file but before our row was written recovers the id instead of uploading twice.
   - A `404` means the session expired: start one fresh session, once.
   - Up to 3 tries per cycle with exponential backoff. The next cycle resumes from the stored session.
4. **Other 4xx** (bad metadata, `forbidden`, `quotaExceeded`, `uploadLimitExceeded`): `state='failed'` with the
   error reason and no automatic retry. `agentpitbench youtube upload --date D` retries by hand.

**Quota** (quota-cost page and `videos.insert` page, both 2026-10-08; revision history 2025-12-04 and 2026-06-01).
Quota days reset at midnight Pacific time.

| Call | Bucket | Cost | Per day (v1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `videos.insert` | Video Uploads: 100 calls/day | 1 | 1 |
| `commentThreads.insert` | default: 10,000 units/day | 50 | 1 → 50 |
| `videos.list` (status + statistics, ≤ 50 ids per call) | default | 1 | ~6 |
| `channels.list` (guard + daily channel stats) | default | 1 | ~2 |
| `videos.update` (only to hide a voided round's film) | default | 50 | ~0 |
| **Total** | | | **1 upload + ~60 of 10,000 units** |

- History: an upload cost about 1,600 units until December 2025, then about 100, and since 2026-06-01 it has its own
  bucket at 1 unit per call. Older guides that say "6 uploads a day" no longer apply.
- A local ledger `yt_quota(day_pt, bucket, units)` counts every call, priced from `youtube_unit_costs` in config.
  `yt-check` prints it. The default bucket has a soft cap of `youtube_units_budget = 2000`: above it, non-upload
  calls skip until the Pacific midnight and log once.

## 4. The upload lock and the compliance audit

There are two separate Google gates. They're often confused, so they're kept apart here.

**(a) OAuth consent screen.**
- An External-type app left in **Testing** gets refresh tokens that **expire after 7 days** (Google OAuth 2.0 doc,
  2026-05-26). The bench would silently lose YouTube weekly.
- So the operator sets the consent screen to **In production**. Unverified is fine for one user: the operator clicks
  through Google's "unverified app" screen once, and the cap is 100 new users (Cloud Help 7454865).
- Google OAuth app verification (needed for sensitive scopes with real users) isn't needed for a single operator.
- If the Cloud project and the channel's managing account are both in the SKALE Labs Workspace, an **Internal** app
  avoids the interstitial and the 7-day rule entirely (operator question 2).

**(b) YouTube API Services audit: is an unaudited project's upload locked private?** The sources disagree. The spec
presents both and picks neither:
- Revision history, entry dated **2020-07-28**: "All videos uploaded via the videos.insert endpoint from unverified API
  projects created after 28 July 2020 will be restricted to private viewing mode. To lift this restriction, each
  project must undergo an audit…" No later entry withdraws it.
- The `videos.insert` and `videos` reference pages, **last updated 2026-10-08**, say verbatim: "Videos uploaded from
  unverified API projects are not restricted to private viewing mode."
- Third-party upload guides dated 2026 (postproxy.dev and others) still describe the lock as enforced, which may just
  reflect the older docs.

**Design that works either way:**
- `youtube_privacy = "private"` by default. Every film is uploaded private.
- The operator reviews it and publishes it by hand in YouTube Studio (one click; about one film a day). The bench
  notices the change on its next `videos.list` and then posts the comment.
- **First-upload test (acceptance step 2):** the operator switches the first film to Public in Studio and watches for
  two things over 24 h: Studio refusing the change, or Google's email saying the video "is locked as private" (the
  revision entry says creators get one). If neither happens, set `youtube_privacy = "public"`, and the films go
  public on upload from then on.
- **If it's locked:** submit the **YouTube API Services Audit and Quota Extension Form** (`support.google.com/youtube/contact/yt_api_form`;
  quota and compliance audits guide, 2026-09-14). The guide gives no timeline ("as soon as possible"), and
  third-party guides say 2–4 weeks. The project must comply with the YouTube API Services Terms and Developer
  Policies. Until the audit passes, keep `"private"` plus the manual publish. The fallback is to upload by hand
  through Studio using the film files and `yt-meta --date D`, which prints the title, description and tags to paste.
- `ytstats.health` flags `locked_private` when a video we've marked public-requested still reads `private` 30 minutes
  after upload (§8).

## 6. Synthetic-content disclosure: not required; `containsSyntheticMedia = false`

- YouTube asks creators to disclose content that is **realistic** and made or meaningfully altered with AI. Its
  examples: making a real person appear to say or do something they didn't, altering footage of a real event or
  place, generating a realistic-looking scene that didn't occur, and AI music that is the focus of the video (Help
  14328491; the same three examples are on the `videos` API doc for `containsSyntheticMedia`, added 2024-10-30).
- Disclosure is **not** required for clearly unrealistic or animated content, or for production assistance
  ("infographics", titles, captions) (same page).
- **The film:**
  - It's an animated data graphic rendered by code from the database: captions, prices, P&L counters, cartoon
    mascots and verbatim text quotes.
  - It shows no real person, no real footage and no realistic generated scene.
  - The quoted transcript lines are AI-written text, shown as attributed quotations of the contestants. That's the
    subject of the record, not synthetic imagery passed off as real.
  - The music is synthesised by a deterministic program (`music.py`), not a generative model, and it sits under the
    captions, not as the video's focus.
- So none of the listed triggers applies. The flag is set explicitly to `false` on every upload, which is a recorded
  decision rather than an omission.
- **Re-check trigger:** a TTS voiceover, AI-generated imagery or likenesses of real people would change this answer.
  v0.4 has none of these, and any of them would need a spec change.

## 7. Analytics (feeding x-analytics-shaped tables)

There are two sources, because the YouTube Analytics API lags **48–72 hours** and truncates `endDate` to the last
fully processed day. Google recommends `videos.list` for real-time per-video numbers (Analytics data-model page and
revision 2026-09-09).

| Source | When | Fields | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| `videos.list?part=statistics,status` | canonical buckets at **1 h, 24 h, 72 h, 7 d** after `published_at`, plus status polling until public | `viewCount`, `likeCount`, `commentCount`; `privacyStatus` | 1 unit per ≤ 50 ids |
| Analytics `reports.query`, `ids=channel==MINE`, `filters=video=={id}` | **7 d and 28 d** after `published_at` | `views`, `engagedViews`, `estimatedMinutesWatched`, `averageViewDuration`, `averageViewPercentage`, `likes`, `comments`, `shares`, `subscribersGained` | 1 query |
| Analytics retention report, `dimensions=elapsedVideoTimeRatio`, `filters=video=={id}` (one video per query) | 7 d and 28 d | `audienceWatchRatio`, `relativeRetentionPerformance` (may be empty on low views) | 1 query |
| `channels.list?part=statistics&mine=true` | daily, first cycle after 00:00 UTC | subscribers, total views, video count | 1 unit |

- Ages run from **`published_at`**, the first time the bench sees the video public, not from upload. A private
  film awaiting the operator would otherwise burn its 1 h bucket.
- The same due rule and "no stale backfill" rule as x-analytics §3 apply.
- **Views:** since 2025-03-31, Shorts `views` count every start and replay, and 2026-08-27 aligned all formats on
  plays from the first frame. `engagedViews` keeps the older "past the first frame or a click to play" count. Both
  are stored, and reports use `engaged_views` as the quality number.
- `averageViewDuration` and `averageViewPercentage` exclude looping traffic (since 2021-12-13).
- `audienceWatchRatio` can exceed 1.0 on Shorts because of replays.
- **Analytics quota:** the YouTube Analytics API has its own quota, separate from the Data API's 10,000 units, and
  Google publishes no cost table for it (each query has a server-computed cost). v1 makes at most **4 `reports.query` calls a day** (one film reaching 7 d and
  one reaching 28 d, two queries each). The operator reads the project's actual limit in Cloud Console at setup
  (§5.7).

**Storage (db.py `SCHEMA`, same conventions as x-analytics §4):**

```sql
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS yt_videos (
    post_key TEXT PRIMARY KEY,          -- motd-<date>: the same key as the X post, so a film joins across platforms
    video_id TEXT,                      -- NULL until the upload completes
    round_id INTEGER, file TEXT, title TEXT,
    privacy_requested TEXT,             -- youtube_privacy at upload
    privacy TEXT,                       -- last status.privacyStatus seen
    state TEXT NOT NULL,                -- uploading | uploaded | failed | outbox | paused | hidden
    upload_session TEXT,                -- resumable session URI while uploading
    uploaded_at REAL, published_at REAL,-- published_at = first seen public
    comment_id TEXT, error TEXT
);
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS yt_metrics (     -- one row per read: the series, like tweet_metrics
    video_id TEXT NOT NULL, taken_at REAL NOT NULL, age_s REAL NOT NULL,
    source TEXT NOT NULL,                   -- 'data' (videos.list) | 'analytics'
    bucket TEXT,                            -- data: 1h|24h|72h|7d; analytics: 7d|28d; NULL = series row
    views INTEGER, likes INTEGER, comments INTEGER,
    engaged_views INTEGER, minutes_watched REAL, avg_view_s REAL, avg_view_pct REAL,
    shares INTEGER, subs_gained INTEGER,
    retention_json TEXT,                    -- [[elapsed_ratio, audience_watch_ratio, relative_perf], …]
    data_through TEXT,                      -- analytics: last fully processed day returned
    raw_json TEXT,
    PRIMARY KEY (video_id, taken_at, source)
);
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS yt_metrics_bucket ON yt_metrics(video_id, source, bucket) WHERE bucket IS NOT NULL;
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS yt_channel_metrics (day TEXT PRIMARY KEY, subscribers INTEGER, views INTEGER, videos INTEGER, taken_at REAL);
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS yt_quota (day_pt TEXT NOT NULL, bucket TEXT NOT NULL, units INTEGER NOT NULL, PRIMARY KEY (day_pt, bucket));
```

**Query layer: `agentpitbench/ytstats.py`.** It's the contract, mirroring `xstats`. These are pure functions over
the DB; `pull_due` is the only one that calls Google. Sibling code imports these functions and writes no SQL against
the tables.

- `snapshot(db, post_key, bucket, source="data") -> dict | None`.
- `video_retention(db, post_key) -> dict | None`: **the same keys as `xstats.video_retention`** (`plays`, `p25`,
  `p50`, `p75`, `p100`, `video_views`, `age_s`, `taken_at`). It reads the latest analytics row:
  - the p-values are `audienceWatchRatio` at elapsed ratios 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0 (nearest point);
  - `plays` = `views`;
  - `video_views` = `engaged_views`.
  - The shape is the same, but the definition differs: X's figures are fractions of playback starts, YouTube's are
    watch ratios that can exceed 1. The digest labels which is which.
- `views_series(db, post_key) -> list[dict]`.
- `reward_inputs(db, post_key, bucket) -> dict | None`: `{views, engaged_views, likes, comments, avg_view_pct, age_s, post_key, round_id, published_at}`.
  It returns None for an unpublished, hidden or outbox video, or a stale row (x-analytics §5's freshness rule and
  ratio). It's provided for later; nothing consumes it in v1 (§9).
- `by_film(db, since) -> list[dict]`: one row per `post_key`. The X side looks up `tweets.tweet_id` by
  `post_key`, because `xstats` functions take a `tweet_id`. It joins X (`xstats.snapshot` 24 h and 7 d impressions,
  video views, `xstats.video_retention`) and YouTube (24 h and 7 d views, engaged views, 7 d avg view %, retention).
  This is the cross-platform table the digest prints.
- `followers_series(db, since=None)`: subscribers per day.
- `quota(db, day_pt=None) -> dict` and `health(db) -> dict`: `{last_ok, consecutive_auth_failures,
  last_upload_state, locked_private: [post_key…], uploads_failed_7d}`, from kv `yt_last_ok` and `yt_auth_failures`
  and the `yt_videos` rows.
- `pull_due(s, db, client, now=None) -> dict`: uploads due, status polls, comment posts, data buckets, analytics
  buckets and the channel row.

## 8. Scheduling, failure handling and pausing

- **Loop:** `_every(600, youtube_cycle, "youtube")` in `cli.run_forever`, calling `ytstats.pull_due` in
  `asyncio.to_thread`. Every HTTP call has a timeout (30 s; 300 s for the PUT).
- The task isn't started unless `youtube_enabled` is true **and** `motd_enabled` is true.
- It's a separate loop, not a call inside `motd.daily`, so a slow or hanging upload can never delay the film's X
  post, rounds or the scheduler.
- **Upload trigger:**
  - Condition: `film.json` exists for today's UTC date (or yesterday's, to cover the 17:00 → midnight edge), and
    `yt_videos` has no row for `motd-<date>`.
  - It doesn't wait on, read or change the X outcome or `kv['motd_<date>']`. A film that failed on X still goes to
    YouTube, and the reverse.
  - No backfill: a film older than yesterday is never uploaded automatically. `youtube upload --date D` does it by hand.
  - So one film produces at most one upload, keyed by `post_key`.
- **Pausing:**
  - `db.paused("posting")` covers both the static `posting_paused` flag and an anomaly pause (ai-automation §10).
    Until the automation build ships `db.paused`, the loop checks `s.posting_paused` directly (a one-line swap
    later). When it's set, the upload and the comment don't happen: write `state='paused'`, log `[paused] yt motd-<date>`,
    and don't replay on resume (the same rule as X). Metrics reads continue, as on X.
  - `dry_run` writes `var/outbox/yt-motd-<date>.json` (title, description, tags, status fields, file path) with
    `state='outbox'` and makes no network calls.
- **Void after filming:** `publish.void_round` gets one added call. If the round has a `yt_videos` row with a
  `video_id`, `videos.update?part=status` sets it private (50 units), with `state='hidden'`. The request re-sends
  every status field the upload set (`selfDeclaredMadeForKids`, `containsSyntheticMedia`, `embeddable`,
  `publicStatsViewable`), because an update replaces the whole part. It's never deleted, so the id stays
  auditable. A failure is logged and never blocks the void.
- **Errors:**
  - Every exception is caught by `_every`'s handler, and the next cycle retries what's retryable (§3).
  - A 401 refreshes the token once. A persistent 401 or 403 increments `yt_auth_failures`, and a success resets it.
  - Nothing here raises into rounds, posting or the site build.
- **Processing and rejection:**
  - The status poll reads `status.uploadStatus` and `status.rejectionReason` (`videos` resource: `claim`,
    `copyright`, `duplicate`, `inappropriate`, `length`, `termsOfUse`, …).
  - Buckets and the comment wait for `uploadStatus = processed`.
  - `rejected` or `failed` sets `state='rejected'` with the reason. A Content ID claim on the CC0 music (§1)
    surfaces here.
- **Alerts (no new alerting code):** ai-automation §10's detector table gets four **warn** rows that read
  `ytstats.health`, batched into the 08:00 UTC digest:
  - `yt-upload-failed`: today's film is `failed`;
  - `yt-auth-failures`: ≥ 3;
  - `yt-locked-private`: a video uploaded with `privacy_requested='public'` still reads `private` after 30 min;
  - `yt-rejected`: a video in `state='rejected'` (with its `rejectionReason`).

  None of them pauses anything.
- **Digest:** the x-analytics daily digest (08:00 UTC, log plus `var/export/x-daily.json`) gains a "YouTube"
  block: yesterday's upload state, `by_film` for the last 7 films, subscribers Δ, and today's quota units.

## 9. Interactions with sibling specs

- **x-analytics:** the same table conventions, due and freshness rules, digest and `post_key` join. `xstats` isn't
  modified, and YouTube reads don't enter the `x_reads` ledger, because they cost nothing in dollars.
- **A/B:** v1 imports nothing from here. The X film post keeps its `generic.cta` arm, and YouTube metadata is the
  control template only. Later, a `yt.title` slot (situations from the film's drama-score signals) could use
  `ab.choose` with the reward `ytstats.reward_inputs` (24 h `engaged_views`, 7 d `avg_view_pct`). At one film a day
  it needs ≥ 40 days for 2 arms, so it isn't worth building before the channel has traffic.
- **AI automation:** four warn detectors (§8). Shorts metrics are an available newsworthiness input via
  `by_film`, under that spec's changelog rule. No opai task writes YouTube text.
- **SEO / ai-referral:** `Organization.sameAs` gains the channel URL (§5.8). No embed, no `VideoObject` change, and
  no new sitemap entries.
- **Share-everywhere:** referrer domains `youtube.com` and `youtu.be` join the weekly referral view.

## 12. Tests (`tests/test_youtube.py`, `tests/test_ytstats.py`; fixtures only, no network)

1. **Metadata:**
   - Title ≤ 100 characters with the question clipped first. The title is built from exactly two parts, `film.json` `hook` + `" | "` +
     the question: no P&L, no "won" or "right", no score.
   - Description ≤ 5000 bytes with multibyte questions. Tags ≤ 500 under the comma and quote counting rule.
   - `<` and `>` stripped.
   - Every number in the description equals the round export's value through the film's helpers.
   - The EXHIBITION line appears only for exhibition rounds.
   - The only URL host is `agentpitbench.org`.
   - The UTM string is exact.
2. **Status fields:** `selfDeclaredMadeForKids=false`, `containsSyntheticMedia=false`, `defaultLanguage="en"`, and
   `privacyStatus` taken from config.
3. **Resumable upload, against a fake server:**
   - `201` on the first PUT;
   - a 503 then a status query returning `308` with `Range: bytes=0-4999`, then a resume from byte 5000;
   - a `308` without `Range` (restart from 0);
   - a `200` on the status query (recovered `video_id`, no second POST);
   - a `404` (one new session);
   - a 400 (`failed`, no retry).
   The session URI is persisted before the PUT.
4. **Idempotency:** two cycles and a restart mid-upload produce one POST per `post_key`.
5. **Isolation:**
   - The `youtube` task raising on every call is caught by `_every`, and the other tasks keep running (including
     `scheduled` → `motd.daily`). `kv['motd_<date>']` is never written by YouTube code.
   - An X failure doesn't stop the upload.
   - No film or `youtube_enabled=false` means no calls.
6. **Pause and dry-run:**
   - `db.paused("posting")` → `state='paused'`, no upload or comment, metrics reads still run.
   - `dry_run` → outbox JSON, no network.
   - A paused film isn't uploaded after resume.
7. **Channel guard:** a mismatched `channels.list` id refuses the upload.
8. **Publishing:**
   - `published_at` is set on the first public sighting, and buckets age from it.
   - The comment is posted once, only when public.
   - The `locked_private` health flag fires after 30 min.
9. **Analytics parsing:** recorded fixtures (anonymised) for the totals report, the retention report and an empty
   `relativeRetentionPerformance` map to the columns. `data_through` is stored.
10. **`video_retention`:** has the same keys as `xstats.video_retention`, with the nearest-point sampling.
11. **`void_round`:** sets a filmed round's video to `hidden` via one `videos.update`, and an update failure doesn't
    raise.
12. **Quota ledger:** costs per call, the Pacific day boundary, and the soft cap skipping non-upload calls.

1. `yt-check` passes: the token refreshes 8 days after login (proving the app isn't in Testing), and the channel
   guard matches.
2. **First-upload test (§4b):** one film is uploaded private with the correct title, description, tags, "not made

   the bench records `published_at` and posts the comment, and within 24 h it's either confirmed not locked

   Short. This is a check that the link path works, not a growth target.
